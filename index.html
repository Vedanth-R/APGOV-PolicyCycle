      Who did what?

      Institution Dashboard

      Use this as the quick civics translation: each actor is tied to the constitutional or bureaucratic route it used.

      WH

      President & White House

      Set the federal agenda through executive orders, the AI Action Plan, procurement priorities, and a push for a national framework.

      C

      Congress

      Adopted a targeted statutory response to deepfake abuse through the TAKE IT DOWN Act, while broader AI preemption remained contested.

      A

      Agencies

      OMB, NIST, FTC, Commerce, Copyright Office, and other agencies translated broad policy into guidance, standards, enforcement, and reports.

      S

      States

      California, Colorado, and many other states tried to fill gaps with transparency, safety, discrimination, and consumer-protection rules.

      J

      Courts

      Federal judges shaped the policy environment through copyright and fair-use decisions, especially around training data and pirated copies.

      P

      Public & Interest Groups

      Victims, creators, tech firms, civil-liberties groups, labor advocates, and state leaders pushed different problem definitions and solutions.

      So what changed?

      Policy Outcomes and Unresolved Conflicts

      Changed

      Federal AI policy moved toward acceleration, procurement, infrastructure, standards, and a narrower set of targeted harms.

      Carried Out

      Agencies began turning executive priorities into memos, compliance plans, standards work, and platform-removal enforcement.

      Contested

      State authority, copyright liability, speech concerns, civil-rights safeguards, and AI model neutrality all remained live fights.
