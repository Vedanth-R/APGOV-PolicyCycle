Which branch of government did what?

Institution Dashboard

Click on a box to get a breakdown of checks/balances, powers used, and an example of legislation

WH

White House/President

Set the federal agenda through executive orders, the AI Action Plan, and a push for international AI dominance.

Power used: Executive orders and media/bully pulpit.

Example action: EO 14179 (removing AI barriers) and America's AI Action Plan.

Check: States and courts can challenge executive actions, and Congress can pass conflicting legislation.

C

Congress

Created a response to deepfake abuse through the TAKE IT DOWN Act, but broader AI topics remained contested.

Power used: Legislation.

Example action: TAKE IT DOWN Act.

Check: Gridlock can limit the scope of legislation, and courts or states can challenge policy.

A

Bureaucracy

OMB, NIST, FTC, Commerce, Copyright Office, and other agencies translated broad policy into standards and enforcement.

Power used: Bureaucratic discretion and implementation.

Example action: FTC enforcement of TAKE IT DOWN obligations and OMB AI use guidance.

Checks: Agencies depend on congressional appropriations and support from the President.

S

States

California, Colorado, and many other states tried to fill gaps with transparency and safety regulations for AI.

Power used: Federalism principles and state regulatory authority.

Example action: California SB 53 and Colorado AI Act implementation planning.

Check: The Supremacy Clause limits state authority over federal policy.

J

Courts

Federal judges shaped the policy environment through their decisions, especially around training data and pirated copies.

Power used: Judicial review and interpretation.

Example action: AI copyright fair-use rulings.

Check: Courts usually move slowly and new legislation can be passed to exploit court loopholes.

P

Public & Interest Groups

Victims, social media creators, tech firms, civil-liberties groups, labor advocates, and state leaders pushed for different approaches.

Power used: Lobbying, public pressure, and litigation.

Example action: Victim advocacy helped push deepfake legislation, and industry groups pushed for innovation approaches.

Limitation: Unequal resources affect whose voice reaches policymakers, especially with AI companies dominating super PACs.

So what changed?

Policy Outcomes and Unresolved Conflicts

What Changed for People

AI policy became more targeted toward real harms people could identify. The TAKE IT DOWN Act connects directly to victims of deepfake abuse by requiring platforms to remove nonconsensual intimate images, including AI-generated ones. Copyright fights connect to writers, musicians, actors, and Hollywood studios because AI companies may train models on books, scripts, voices, performances, and artwork. State laws like California SB 53 connect to consumers by pushing transparency around powerful AI systems.

What It Means for Students (like us in AP GOV)

For us, AI and policies have affected how schools define cheating, how teachers create assignments, how we learn with AI tools, and what skills matter in future jobs. The unresolved conflict is that AI can help students study and brainstorm, but schools also have to encourage real thinking skills and original work. For me and Cedric, we have to think about the future when deciding on college and what skills we even might need later on.
